# Burgundy theme (Hindi mode)

When the Hindi switch on Home is on, the whole app changes language, dates and colour. This page is the colour part: the palette, how it
is wired, how danger stays apart from the burgundy brand colour, and the measured WCAG 2.1 contrast of every text and control pair.

## Wiring

- `IncidentTheme(darkTheme = true, hindi = false, content)` in `ui/theme/Theme.kt` picks one of four `IncidentColors` sets
  (`LightColors`, `DarkColors`, `HindiLightColors`, `HindiDarkColors`) and builds the Material colour scheme from it.
- `MainActivity` collects `vm.ui` with `collectAsStateWithLifecycle()` and passes `hindiLane` as `hindi`. The theme is a
  parameter, so it switches the moment the switch is flipped, with no restart and no recreated activity.
- Screens read `Theme.colors.*` only, so no screen needed to change. The default stays dark.
- The English palette is untouched (the checker reports it, it does not edit it).

## Danger and simulated stay apart from burgundy

Burgundy is a dark red-purple, and the English red danger and magenta simulated colours would sit next to it as near-neighbours.
Three things keep them apart:

1. **Different hue.** Danger moves to vermilion (orange-red) and simulated to violet, so neither shares burgundy's hue.
   The checker measures CIELAB distance and hue angle between accent, danger and simulated, and exits 1 if two
   colours are both close in hue and close in lightness.
2. **Different lightness.** Light mode: burgundy is very dark (L* 28) and danger a brighter vermilion (L* 42). Dark mode: burgundy
   becomes a pale rose (L* 80) and danger a more saturated orange (L* 67).
3. **Never colour alone.** As before, danger is always an icon plus a word (the Needed pill has a warning icon and "Needed"; the
   mic button swaps its microphone glyph for a stop glyph and its accessible name becomes "Stop recording"; the SIMULATED strip
   has a warning icon and the word).

## Brand colours

The amber evidence band (`#F2BE72`) and its letter colour (`#0A4F49`) are fixed in `BrandMark.kt` and `res/values/colors.xml` and are
unchanged. They are not palette roles. On the mark, the band sits on the speech bubble (`onAccent`), never directly on burgundy: in
light mode the bubble is near-white, in dark mode it is the very dark burgundy `#3B0A1D`. The letters are dark teal on amber at
5.56:1 in both modes, so the mark stays legible. The splash screen (shown before the app knows the language) keeps its teal.

Known leftovers outside the files this change owns: `ui/ProofStrip.kt` hard-codes four compute colours (`computeColor`), and its
SIMULATED pink `#D81B60` is the one hard-coded colour that sits near burgundy in hue. It is always labelled and iconned, but it
should move to `Theme.colors.simulated` in a follow-up. `CapturePalette` (green capture panel) is defined but currently unused.

## Contrast (WCAG 2.1, measured)

Generated by `python3 tools/theme_contrast_check.py --markdown`, which parses the hex values straight out of `Tokens.kt`.
Thresholds: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and UI components (outlines, focus ring, progress, slider, solid fills). Disabled
controls are exempt from WCAG 1.4.3 and are not listed. Every pair passes in all four palettes. The first two tables are the
existing English palette, for comparison; the Hindi palettes were tuned until nothing failed (the first draft of danger was
slightly closer to burgundy in hue and was moved further toward orange).

Re-run after any token change; it exits 1 on a failing pair:

```
python3 tools/theme_contrast_check.py
```
