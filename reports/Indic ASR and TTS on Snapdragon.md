# Fine-tuned Whisper weights unlock offline Indic speech

**No open-weight model covers English, Hindi and Telugu speech on the Hexagon v79 NPU out of the box, and the one pre-built Indic route the notes surface, stock multilingual Whisper, is a trap for Telugu.** Stock Whisper-small scores **98.2% WER on Telugu and 38.4% on Hindi** (FLEURS), and even medium reaches only 82.8% / 26.8% ([Whisper paper, Table 13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356)) [V]. The workable NPU path is therefore the Whisper architecture with fine-tuned Indic weights: vasista22's Telugu tiny/base/small reach **17.2 / 13.4 / 11.6% FLEURS WER** and collabora's Hindi tiny/base/small reach **10.1 / 8.5 / 7.2%** (Whisper normalization; roughly double under Indic normalization) [V]. Whether Qualcomm AI Hub's exporter accepts these checkpoints end to end is unverified [I], so the hedge is AI4Bharat's IndicConformer-120M (about **138 MB int8 ONNX per language**, MIT) on the CPU. English stays on the NPU with Qualcomm's English Whisper, ideally base.en. For TTS, **nothing Indic exists on the NPU anywhere** [V]. The practical stack is open-weight Piper `hi_IN`/`te_IN` voices through sherpa-onnx on the CPU, or Google's offline `hi-IN`/`te-IN`/`en-IN` voice packs, disclosed to judges as CPU. No public Android project has shown Hindi or Telugu ASR or TTS on a Hexagon NPU, so the team would be first and should plan to debug alone [V].

Notation follows the notes: **[V]** was verified in a model card, API, paper, repo or on the kit; **[I]** is inferred. Hindi/Telugu WERs from different sources are not directly comparable, because normalization and in-domain training data change the number by up to 2x.

## Stock Whisper fails Telugu, so accuracy decides the model

The accuracy picture splits cleanly by language. For English, Whisper degrades about 3x on Indian-accented speech: on Svarah (9.6 h, 117 speakers) Whisper base scores **13.6%** against 4.2% on LibriSpeech, medium 8.3% and large 7.2%, while NVIDIA's 120M Conformer-CTC scores 14.6% ([Svarah](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15760)) [V]. No primary source gives a Svarah number for tiny.en, so the team's current model is probably in the high teens [I], and base.en or small.en is the cheapest English gain. Stock English FLEURS WER runs tiny 12.4, base 8.9, small 6.1 ([Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356)) [V].

For Hindi and Telugu, two families are good enough. The first is fine-tuned Whisper. vasista22's Telugu medium and large-v2 reach 9.5 and 9.7% FLEURS ([small card](https://huggingface.co/vasista22/whisper-telugu-small)) [V], and collabora's Hindi models are CC-BY-4.0 ([small-hindi](https://huggingface.co/collabora/whisper-small-hindi)) [V]. These numbers are optimistic: vasista22 trained on FLEURS train and dev, and a small-data Telugu tiny fine-tune from another author still scores 97.8% ([card](https://huggingface.co/vanshnawander/whisper-tiny-telugu)) [V]. Realistic spontaneous-speech Hindi for tiny/small fine-tunes is nearer 18 to 30% on Kathbath-style sets ([ARTPARK small](https://huggingface.co/ARTPARK-IISc/whisper-small-vaani-hindi)) [V for the numbers; I for the "more realistic" reading]. The second family is IndicConformer. The 600M multilingual model scores **Hindi 13.8 and Telugu 26.4** averaged over the SraVaani paper's datasets ([arXiv 2608.08235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08235)) [V], and on unscripted telephonic speech 8.2 / 23.7 ([Voice of India](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151v1)) [V]. The 120M per-language models have no published WER, though the 130M IndicVoices baseline of the same class scored Hindi 15.0 / Telugu 26.8 ([IndicVoices](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01926)) [V]. IndicConformer has no English and needs the language code up front ([card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual)) [V].

Two more facts shape the choice. And Qwen3-ASR, Moonshine, SenseVoice and Gemma 3n are out: the first has Hindi but no Telugu, the next two have no Indic support, and Gemma 3n's Indic ASR quality is undocumented ([Qwen3-ASR](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B), [Gemma audio docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/capabilities/audio)) [V]. Vosk's 58 MB Telugu model scores 87.9% WER and is useless ([Vosk](https://alphacephei.com/vosk/models)) [V].

## The NPU route to Hindi and Telugu runs through custom Whisper graphs

Four deployment routes exist for ASR on the v79 NPU, and they differ sharply in what they can reach.

**WhisperKit Android** is English-only on the NPU. It is archived and deprecated (last release 0.3.3), exposes `QUALCOMM_TINY_EN` and `QUALCOMM_BASE_EN`, has no language or task API, and users report multilingual output being translated to English ([repo](https://github.com/argmaxinc/WhisperKitAndroid), [issue #75](https://github.com/argmaxinc/WhisperKitAndroid/issues/75)) [V]. It does run on an SM8750 QIDK: a third-party test on Android 15 passed the NPU transcription in 4.8 s against 0.7 s on CPU, probably dominated by graph preparation, so the model must be warmed at app start ([sm8750-edge-ai-lab](https://github.com/AryamaVMurthy/sm8750-edge-ai-lab/blob/HEAD/android-app/app/build.gradle.kts)) [V for the timings; I for the cause]. Its models download from pinned old commits of Qualcomm's deprecated HF repos, which is fragile; bundle them offline [V; the advice is I]. Open issues report "!!!!" output and QNN error 6001 on SM8750 with older QNN versions ([issue #58](https://github.com/argmaxinc/WhisperKitAndroid/issues/58)) [V].

**sherpa-onnx with the QNN backend** is the broadest open route. It publishes prebuilt SM8750 context binaries for multilingual Whisper tiny (104 MB), base (176 MB), small (507 MB) and medium (1.48 GB), and its QNN Whisper code honours `language=hi|te` ([release](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/tag/asr-models-qnn-binary-2), [source](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/blob/master/sherpa-onnx/csrc/qnn/offline-whisper-model-qnn.cc)) [V]. The catches are that you must build the native libraries with QNN yourself (no QNN AAR or prebuilt Whisper APK exists), models are fixed-shape and padded or truncated to 5 to 30 s, and the prebuilt binaries are stock weights, so they inherit the Telugu failure above ([build doc](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/qnn/build-android-demo.html), [models doc](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/qnn/models.html)) [V]. The QAIRT community SDK downloads without a login ([download-qnn](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/qnn/download-qnn.html)) [V].

**Qualcomm AI Hub assets** supply public, login-free context binaries for multilingual Whisper tiny through large-v3-turbo on "Snapdragon 8 Elite For Galaxy" (QAIRT 2.50). Whisper-Small float runs **65 ms per 30 s encoder window and 8.3 ms per decoder token**, 34 / 4.7 ms in w8a16; Tiny is 14 / 1.6 ms ([Whisper-Small](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/Whisper-Small), [Whisper-Small-Quantized](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/Whisper-Small-Quantized)) [V]. At roughly 60 tokens that is about 0.56 s, so speed is not the constraint and accuracy should pick the model [I]. The exporter takes any HF Whisper id and advertises custom weights, but nobody has verified a vasista22 or collabora checkpoint through it [V for the API; I for the end-to-end claim] ([model.py](https://github.com/qualcomm/ai-hub-models/blob/main/src/qai_hub_models/models/templates/hf_whisper/model.py)). Compiling your own is free, but the "Snapdragon 8 Elite" device and the `qnn_context_binary` target were retired in September 2026; target Galaxy S25 and use the compile-plus-link workflow, so older tutorials fail ([release notes](https://workbench.aihub.qualcomm.com/docs/hub/release_notes.html)) [V]. The reference decode loop auto-detects the language and caps decoding at 200 tokens, so a custom loop must seed `[SOT, <|te|>, <|transcribe|>, <|notimestamps|>]` and keep utterances to 5 to 10 s, because Whisper's byte-level BPE spends many tokens per Indic word ([app.py](https://github.com/qualcomm/ai-hub-models/blob/main/src/qai_hub_models/models/templates/hf_whisper/app.py)) [V for the loop and cap; I for the truncation risk]. Silent wrong output on Hexagon has documented precedents, so every NPU transcript should be A/B-checked against a CPU reference on a fixed WAV [V; the A/B check is I].

**The Qualcomm Voice AI ASR SDK** (the route behind the QIDK ASR-LLM-TTS sample) is QPM-login-gated, auto-detects language and exposes no language setter in the sample, so it is unsuitable for short code-mixed utterances unless a forcing API turns up ([sample](https://github.com/qualcomm/qidk/blob/master/GenAI-Solutions/ASR-LLM-TTS/sample_app/app/src/main/java/com/example/asr_llm_tts/asr/Whisper_ASR.java)) [V; the suitability call is I].

What about IndicConformer on the NPU? Fixed-length Conformer encoders already run fully on the 8 Elite NPU in AI Hub (FunASR-Conformer 22.9 ms per 10 s; Wav2Vec2-Conformer-Large 768 ms per 10 s), and ONNX Runtime's QNN EP supports v79 with `soc_model=SM8750`, but it needs static shapes and has never been demonstrated for IndicConformer ([FunASR card](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/FunASR-Conformer-EN), [ORT-QNN docs](https://github.com/onnxruntime/onnxruntime-qnn/blob/main/docs/execution_providers/QNN-ExecutionProvider.md)) [V; feasibility is I]. Treat NPU IndicConformer as a stretch goal and ship it on the CPU through onnxruntime or onnx-asr first. The kit has **15.4 GB of RAM**, so the 1.1 GB peak of the int8 600M model is affordable, a constraint the ASR notes assumed was tighter ([local kit notes](file:///home/th4kur/Desktop/m26-qidk/.claude/skills/qidk-npu-android/SKILL.md)) [V].

## Indic TTS stays on the CPU, and Piper is the open-weight answer

Every TTS route that reaches the NPU is English or European. Qualcomm publishes only MeloTTS EN/ES/ZH and PiperTTS EN/DE/IT, shipped for the Voice AI SDK, and the shared language enum has no Hindi or Telugu ([MeloTTS-EN](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/MeloTTS-EN), [voiceai_tts/language.py](https://github.com/qualcomm/ai-hub-models/blob/main/src/qai_hub_models/models/templates/voiceai_tts/language.py)) [V]. MeloTTS has an **EN_INDIA** speaker, making it the only NPU-ready Indian-accented English voice (about 105 ms of NPU time per call on 8 Elite), but nobody confirmed that Qualcomm's pack exposes that speaker ([MeloTTS](https://github.com/myshell-ai/MeloTTS)) [V for the speaker upstream; I for the pack]. No open model under about 100M parameters covers all three languages; every single-model option is 350M to 3.8B or misses a language ([Indic Parler-TTS](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts), [IndicF5](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/IndicF5)) [V].

Piper wins for three reasons. It is open, tiny and the only family with both Hindi and Telugu voices at phone scale (Telugu voices added 2025-08-26). On a Galaxy S10, sherpa-onnx's Piper low voices ran at RTF 0.076 against Android system TTS at 0.058 ([VoicePing](https://voiceping.net/en/blog/research-offline-tts-eval/)) [V], and the 8 Elite's cores are much faster, so medium voices should land well under RTF 0.3 [I]. And sherpa-onnx already ships Hindi engine APKs that the existing `TextToSpeech` code can select by package name [V; the integration is I]. Two caveats apply. Piper hi/te rely on espeak-ng phonemes, MMS hin/tel vocabularies contain no Latin letters, and Supertonic and SYSPIN are character-based, so the app must store prompts in native script and expand numbers, dates, acronyms and English loanwords into native-script words before synthesis [V for the vocabularies; I for the fix]. The hackathon-sized work is a small number-to-words function per language [I]. The second caveat is that Piper has no `en_IN` voice, so English comes from Piper en_GB, Google `en-IN`, or MeloTTS on the NPU.

On Google TTS, the kit's voice manifest lists `en-in` (16.2 MB), `hi-in` (17.6 MB) and `te-in` (11.0 MB) packs, but only `en-us` is installed and the kit is in airplane mode, so the packs need a one-time online install and an airplane-mode test before the demo ([Google help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/6006983?hl=en)) [V for the manifest; offline synthesis after install is I, untested]. Large Orpheus-style models (Svara 3.3B, Indic-Speak 3.3B, Veena 3.8B) are not real-time on a phone, but Indic Parler-TTS, Svara or Indic-Speak can pre-render the fixed follow-up prompts on a laptop as bundled WAVs, a polish trick that must be disclosed as pre-recorded synthetic audio ([Svara](https://huggingface.co/kenpath/svara-tts-v1)) [V; the trick is I].

An NPU Indic TTS is a stretch, not a plan. AI Hub's Piper template already splits VITS into encoder, SDP, flow and decoder graphs with fixed shapes, deterministic noise buffers and a chunked decoder, and it loads one voice straight from a released Piper ONNX, so a Hindi or Telugu entry is conceivable. Its bundled Charsiu G2P has no Telugu training data, so G2P would stay on the CPU with espeak-ng ([template](https://github.com/qualcomm/ai-hub-models/blob/main/src/qai_hub_models/models/templates/pipertts/model.py), [CharsiuG2P](https://github.com/lingjzhu/CharsiuG2P)) [V; viability is I, unverified]. Kokoro on the HTP has only been shown for English on SM8650 with heavy surgery ([kokoro-offline-tts-android](https://github.com/cedgeremek/kokoro-offline-tts-android)) [V].

## Priority-ordered plan with fallbacks

The order below optimizes for an honest, demoable three-language pipeline within 24 hours, then climbs toward more NPU. Each step names the trigger for falling back, and the timings are my estimates [I].

| Priority | Item | Primary path | Fallback | Runs on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | English ASR | WhisperKit `QUALCOMM_TINY_EN`, then `BASE_EN` with `com.qualcomm.qti:qnn-runtime:2.44.0` + `qnn-litert-delegate:2.44.0`, models bundled offline | CPU `OPENAI_TINY_EN`; AI Hub Whisper-Small via ORT-QNN with a Kotlin decode loop | NPU |
| 2 | Hindi and Telugu ASR, 30-minute spike | Run 50 clips each of Kathbath/IndicVoices through IndicConformer-120M int8 (OpenVoiceOS ONNX), vasista22/collabora Whisper, and Omnilingual-300M; pick on measured WER | If none clears about 25% WER, use IndicConformer-600M int8 for hi+te | CPU |
| 3 | Move the Whisper winner to the NPU | Export the chosen fine-tuned Whisper (base or small) via AI Hub custom weights (target Galaxy S25, compile+link) with forced `<|hi|>`/`<|te|>`; run through ORT-QNN or sherpa-onnx QNN | If it fails or A/B-diverges from CPU, ship IndicConformer on the CPU and label it | NPU, else CPU |
| 4 | Language selection | Explicit EN/HI/TE toggle that sets the ASR engine, TTS voice and LLM prompt language | None needed | n/a |
| 5 | Open-weight TTS | sherpa-onnx Piper `hi_IN` (rohan or priyamvada), `te_IN` (padmavathi or venkatesh, converted), plus Piper en or MMS-eng | Google TTS packs for en-IN/hi-IN/te-IN (installed online first, tested in airplane mode) | CPU |
| 6 | Pre-rendered prompts | Fixed follow-up questions rendered with Indic Parler-TTS in hi/te/en-IN, bundled as WAV | Skip | n/a |
| 7 | NPU stretch | MeloTTS-EN (EN_INDIA) via Voice AI TTS SDK; or AI Hub Piper template adapted to `hi_IN`/`te_IN`; or IndicConformer through ORT-QNN at fixed 10 s windows | Drop | NPU |

Step 3 is the pivotal bet. If the AI Hub export accepts the fine-tuned weights, the team gets a genuinely NPU-resident Hindi and Telugu ASR whose only extras are tokenizer and mel on the CPU; if not, the honest fallback is CPU IndicConformer with the NPU doing English ASR and the LLM [I]. The sherpa-onnx prebuilt binaries are a distraction for Telugu: their stock weights fail, and whether the sherpa export scripts accept a custom HF checkpoint was not checked. Present the split with a live execution-provider badge and per-stage timings, as the Sahaay and Kokoro-HTP projects do, and never claim "fully NPU" while TTS runs on the CPU ([Sahaay](https://github.com/andringodson/Hackathon-SnapdragonAILab), [local kit notes](file:///home/th4kur/Desktop/m26-qidk/.claude/skills/qidk-npu-android/SKILL.md)) [V for the practice; the application is I].

## Where the notes disagree or fall short

The most important conflict is between the NPU notes and the ASR notes. The NPU notes name multilingual Whisper-small or medium with `language=hi|te` as the fastest Hindi/Telugu route and note that no Indic WER was found for any AI Hub asset. The ASR notes supply exactly that missing number, and it is damning for Telugu (98.2% small, 82.8% medium, 99.0% large-v2) and weak for Hindi (38.4% small, 26.8% medium, 21.5% large-v2) ([Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356)) [V]. The reconciled reading is that the NPU notes' runtime route is right and their model choice is wrong. Whisper large-v3 and v3-turbo per-language numbers for hi/te were not found anywhere, so the turbo builds' Telugu quality is unknown, though large-v2's 99.0% makes a big improvement unlikely [I].

A second gap is the fine-tune-swap itself. The ASR notes assume the fine-tuned base and small checkpoints are drop-in compatible with the NPU graphs because they share the stock architecture and tokenizer [I]; the NPU notes verify only that the exporter takes any HF Whisper id [V]. Neither tested it. A third gap is the Qualcomm PiperTTS-EN card's "Max decoded sequence length: 64 tokens" against the template's 512-phoneme maximum and 40-frame decoder chunks ([card](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/PiperTTS-EN), [template](https://github.com/qualcomm/ai-hub-models/blob/main/src/qai_hub_models/models/templates/pipertts/model.py)) [V for both]; these may measure different things, but the notes do not reconcile them.

The notes also differ on environment. The TTS and NPU notes report the kit as Android 16 over an Android 15 vendor image with 15.4 GB RAM, while the user's brief and the spec say Android 15 and leave RAM unstated ([local kit notes](file:///home/th4kur/Desktop/m26-qidk/.claude/skills/qidk-npu-android/SKILL.md)) [V for the measurement]. The verified WhisperKit run was on Android 15, so re-verify on the kit's actual image. The ASR notes' "8 GB device" memory worry, drawn from a community model card, is moot at 15.4 GB. Finally, one unverified detail in the ASR notes (a search-snippet Voice of India figure of 6.5/20.0 for IndicConformer, against the paper's 8.2/23.7) was left out of the table because the paper text is the better source ([voiceofindia.ai](https://voiceofindia.ai/)) [unverified].

No Indic model has a measured latency or WER on a Snapdragon NPU or CPU in any source, and no TTS has a measured RTF on SM8750 [V as gaps]. Every accuracy number above is therefore a published benchmark number, not a kit measurement.

## What changes in the current plan

The spec's D9 pairs `com.argmaxinc:whisperkit:0.3.3` with `com.qualcomm.qnn:qnn-runtime:2.34.0` and `com.qualcomm.qnn:qnn-litert-delegate:2.34.0`. That group returns **404** on Maven Central, which holds only `com.qualcomm.qti`; both artifacts exist there (qnn-runtime 2.26.0 to 2.51.0, litert-delegate 2.28.0 to 2.51.0) ([README](https://github.com/argmaxinc/WhisperKitAndroid), [qti metadata](https://repo1.maven.org/maven2/com/qualcomm/qti/qnn-runtime/maven-metadata.xml)) [V]. Change the coordinates to `com.qualcomm.qti:*:2.44.0`, the version proven on SM8750, and correct the same lines in the `qidk-npu-android` skill and the implementation plan, which repeat the wrong group [V for the match in those files; the version choice is I]. Also note that the NPU-proven profile was `qualcomm/Whisper_Tiny_En`, which WhisperKit exposes as `QUALCOMM_TINY_EN`, whereas D9 lists `OPENAI_TINY_EN`, the profile the verified test ran on CPU [V for the test; I for the implication]. Switch D9's first model to `QUALCOMM_TINY_EN`, then `QUALCOMM_BASE_EN`.

WhisperKit is English-only, so it can no longer be the whole of D9 and D10. D9 becomes the English engine only. D10 (Voice AI ASR via QPM) should be demoted: it is gated and auto-detects language. Hindi and Telugu need a new decision covering the path in priorities 2 and 3, with the explicit language toggle that Section 5's "out of scope: multilingual input" currently excludes. That exclusion must be lifted.

D11 keeps Android `TextToSpeech` as the zero-risk baseline but needs a concrete install step: download the `hi-IN`, `te-IN` and `en-IN` packs online, then verify `getVoices()` returns installed, `isNetworkConnectionRequired()==false` voices in airplane mode. Add open-weight sherpa-onnx Piper as the preferred voice and keep the "disclose CPU" rule. The knock-on changes the notes do not cover but the new scope forces are that the follow-up questions, the `EnumNormalizer` synonyms and the `DateTimeResolver` must handle Hindi and Telugu, the LLM must read Telugu script (not yet checked with the chosen Qwen3/Gemma models), and the D7 evidence-span check must run on native-script transcripts [I].

## Conclusion

The central lesson is that the NPU's ASR constraint is the weights, not the hardware. Qualcomm's runtimes will run Whisper on the v79 NPU in tens of milliseconds, but the Whisper that Qualcomm and sherpa-onnx ship is the one that gets Telugu wrong, so the leverage is a 30-minute accuracy spike followed by a custom export, not more runtime plumbing. TTS tells the opposite story: the models are fine and small, but Qualcomm's NPU TTS pipeline has no Hindi or Telugu front end, so honesty about CPU execution is cheaper and safer than a heroic port.

The strategic implication is a mixed-execution demo in which the NPU carries English ASR, the LLM and, if the export works, Hindi and Telugu Whisper, while open-weight TTS and a possible CPU IndicConformer are labelled as CPU with live evidence. Because no public Android project has run Hindi or Telugu speech on Hexagon, a working NPU Whisper in either language would itself be a differentiator, and a clearly labelled CPU hedge costs little in score if it does not land.
